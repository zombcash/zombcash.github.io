THE MEMECOIN BATTLEFIELD

CoinSniper vs CoinMooner vs CoinVote. Three platforms promise to put a new token in front of the crypto crowd. But a listing, a vote, a boost, a click and an actual human who cares are five very different species.

RESEARCH DATE: 3 NOVEMBER 2026 · RESEARCH SCOPE: PUBLIC PAGES, PLATFORM DOCUMENTATION, LEGAL DOCUMENTS, THIRD-PARTY TRAFFIC ESTIMATES AND A ZOMBCASH FIELD REVIEW.

MISSION BRIEF: This is an independent editorial comparison, not sponsored content and not an endorsement by any of the platforms. Platform-reported audience and listing figures are labelled as self-reported. Traffic estimates are not first-party analytics. No metric here proves that a visitor bought a token.

00 · The short version: three different battle doctrines

CoinSniper

The radar station. A broad token-discovery directory with votes, Boosts, watchlists, KYC, audits, advertising and quests. Its model is visibility plus optional credibility services.

Visit CoinSniper ↗ · @coinsniper_net

CoinMooner

The advertising bazaar. A wide crypto directory with presales, audited and doxxed filters, most-viewed pages, banners, pop-ups, promoted spots, email blasts and contract audits.

Visit CoinMooner ↗ · @CoinMoonerX

CoinVote

The community barracks. Free listings and votes, member profiles, posts, likes, Nuggets, streaks, challenges, Boosts, mobile apps and a community hub. It makes repeated participation part of the product.

Visit CoinVote ↗ · @coinvote_cc

Editorial thesis: CoinVote deserves special recognition for turning promotion into ongoing community participation. CoinSniper offers a broad discovery and verification toolbox. CoinMooner offers a large directory and a conventional advertising menu. The platforms are not interchangeable, and their headline counters do not measure the same thing.

01 · Origins: who built the bunkers?

CoinSniper — the founder is visible; the corporate operator is not

CoinSniper's domain registration dates to 14 May 2021. A Nasdaq-distributed press release dated 30 April 2022 announced CookieSale as CoinSniper's exclusive multi-chain launchpad partner and described CoinSniper as a voting platform where users could list and discover new crypto projects. The announcement is a useful dated milestone, not proof of a market-leading position.

The platform's own terms describe a cryptocurrency directory and discovery service. However, the public Terms and Privacy Policy reviewed for this dossier identify the operator as “CoinSniper” without clearly naming a registered legal entity, registration number or country of incorporation. Its terms refer to the laws of the place of establishment but do not identify that place. The domain's WHOIS privacy service is not proof of wrongdoing; it simply does not answer the corporate-identity question.

Development is visible in its feature history: the Boost system, project-owner dashboards, KYC and audit options, and a Quests feature. The 2022 CookieSale partnership shows that CoinSniper's ambitions extended beyond a list of token cards.

CoinMooner — a 2021 listing project that grew into a crypto portal

CoinMooner describes its beginning as a coin-listing platform in April 2021. Its present website is much broader: listings, presales, airdrops, games, price predictions, jobs, events and a multi-category crypto blog. Its directory covers multiple chains, including Solana, Ethereum and BNB Chain.

CoinMooner also has a native BNB Chain token, MOONER. Historical token information and an October 2021 audit document show that the token existed early in the project's life. That is a separate question from whether MOONER currently has meaningful exchange liquidity: third-party token trackers report a lack of reliable live trading data. The site itself should not be confused with the market performance of its token.

Its current legal pages name two operating entities: COINMOONER OÜ in Estonia and SIA OK WEB in Latvia. The Estonian company register aggregator reports COINMOONER OÜ entered liquidation in March 2026; Latvian company records show SIA OK WEB as active. CoinMooner's “Operating Entities” page was last updated in August 2024 and its terms in February 2024. That mismatch is a legitimate due-diligence question: before paying, ask which entity is currently contracting and invoicing. It is not evidence that the website has stopped operating.

CoinVote — a 2021 voting site that added a social layer

CoinVote says it was founded in 2021 by Léo Mesbah in France. Its public “About” page identifies the operator and explains its ranking mechanics. In October 2026 the platform introduced a major V3-era community layer: member rewards, daily streaks, challenges, Boosts, posts and replies in the Community Hub, plus mobile-app features and Telegram-related integrations.

The important development is conceptual. CoinVote did not stop at “submit a token, collect votes, disappear.” It built reasons for members to return and interact. A daily vote is still a vote, but a vote connected to a profile, streak, reward balance, post, reply or community identity is a richer engagement loop.

Analytics rule: we will not convert a Tranco rank into visits, an “upvote” into a unique person, a page view into a lead, or a platform's “monthly active users” into verified token buyers. Those are different units, and pretending otherwise is how marketing decks learn to levitate.

04 · Engagement: where the numbers become people

CoinVote's strongest differentiator is a measurable participation loop

CoinVote's public About page reports more than 427,000 member accounts, roughly 5.62 million votes since launch and about 34,000 active projects in the November 2026 snapshot. The current daily ranking page shows hundreds of votes in a 24-hour window and projects receiving those votes. Its own activity report indicates that Solana projects accounted for approximately 68% of votes in the sampled 24-hour period. These are platform-reported counters, not audited unique humans, but they are more directly tied to platform participation than a generic “monthly reach” claim.

CoinVote combines several engagement mechanics:

  • Daily votes: members can support up to three different projects per day, one vote per project.
  • Nuggets: reward points earned through participation such as voting, daily visits and challenges. The platform says Nuggets cannot be purchased.
  • Streaks and challenges: repeated visits and tasks give users a reason to return.
  • Boosts: members can spend earned Nuggets on project visibility; projects can also buy Boost packs.
  • Community Hub: project and member posts, replies, likes and profile-based interactions create a social layer around discovery.
  • Mobile apps: iOS and Android distribution reduces the need to revisit the website manually.

That combination is why CoinVote gets special credit in this study. It does not merely sell a slot on a page; it gives members an ongoing role in the discovery process. A community member can vote, earn points, keep a streak, post, reply and return. For a small project, that is a more useful starting point than buying an impression with no follow-up path.

CoinSniper: large discovery community, but votes are not the whole ranking

CoinSniper displays large cumulative counters for projects, votes, watchlists, KYC and audits. Its Boost system changes the ranking economics: Boosts can carry priority over ordinary votes. The platform introduced this model after explaining that projects could accumulate large vote totals and obtain an unfair advantage. That is a sensible design problem to acknowledge, but it also means a team must understand the paid and unpaid parts of the ranking before interpreting a position.

CoinSniper also has a Telegram community and an announcements channel. The main Telegram group has shown around 19,000 members in a public snapshot, while the announcements channel is much smaller. These counts establish an audience footprint, not how many people click through to a specific token or buy it.

CoinMooner: discovery signals, views and a broad crypto content ecosystem

CoinMooner offers Most Viewed, Most Upvoted, New Coins, Trending, Presales and other browsing routes. Its Ads page claims 50 million+ upvotes and 1 million+ monthly active users; those are the platform's own marketing claims and have not been independently verified in this dossier. The directory exposes vote counts and view-based sorting, which can help a project appear in more than one discovery path.

The distinction matters: an upvote is a platform action, a view is a page load or recorded view, and an active user is a platform-defined metric. Without a published methodology, we should not treat them as interchangeable.

08 · Technical reliability: bugs, loading and the difference between a glitch and a verdict

A fair reliability comparison requires repeated, timestamped browser tests from more than one network, device and region. We did not obtain a defensible multi-day uptime sample for any of the three platforms. Therefore this dossier does not assign a reliability score or claim that any platform is consistently unstable.

  • CoinSniper: public announcement history includes maintenance notices warning that some features could be offline during a site upgrade and inviting users to report bugs. Some captured Boost-page results also failed to display package details during research. A transient page or extraction failure is not proof of a sustained outage; it is a reason to recheck before payment.
  • CoinMooner: the main coin directory and advertising page were readable in the research snapshot. Its legal pages are older than the current company-status records, and some legal-page metadata returned generic default title/description values. This is a documentation/metadata observation, not proof that the interactive site freezes.
  • CoinVote: the live ranking, About and legal pages expose detailed rules and current counters. Some automated retrieval attempts returned cache misses; those failures came from the retrieval layer and are not counted as website downtime. No reproducible user-facing freeze was established in this research.
How to read this section: “not observed” does not mean “never happens.” We distinguish a confirmed site message, a single failed capture, a third-party extraction problem and a reproducible browser defect. Only the last category justifies a firm UX bug report.

For a paid campaign, run a pre-flight check on the exact project page, checkout, ad preview and mobile layout. Save screenshots, note the time and browser, and get written confirmation of placement dates and any make-good policy.

09 · Trust, moderation and legal anatomy

CoinSniper

CoinSniper publishes Terms and a Privacy Policy. The reviewed terms state that user-submitted project information may be unverified and that the platform is not an exchange or financial adviser. The policy discusses data processing and KYC data handling. The weakness for a business buyer is not that the documents are absent; it is that the public documents reviewed did not clearly identify the legal company, its registration number or a precise governing jurisdiction. Paid services also have restrictive refund language once work has started. Ask for the invoice issuer and legal counterparty before sending funds.

CoinMooner

CoinMooner publishes Terms and Conditions, Privacy Policy, Cookie Statement, Disclaimer and an Operating Entities page. The Terms identify COINMOONER OÜ and SIA OK WEB as the companies operating the site and say both are responsible for the site and personal-data processing. The Terms were last updated February 17, 2024; the Operating Entities page says August 2024. Public Estonian registry data show COINMOONER OÜ in liquidation from March 2026, while Latvian records show SIA OK WEB active. This makes it essential to verify which entity currently signs advertising contracts, issues invoices and handles disputes. The older terms state that sales are final and no refund is issued. The privacy policy describes data rights for EEA users, but it too should be checked for current accuracy.

CoinVote

CoinVote's public legal pages identify Léo MESBAH EI, trading as Coinvote, registered in France. The privacy policy names its data controller, explains account/community data, payment processors, cookie consent, security signals used to reduce vote abuse and GDPR rights. Its Terms prohibit bots, vote farming and duplicate project listings. CoinVote says it does not audit project contracts or teams; a listing is not an endorsement. Its sales terms describe consumer withdrawal rights and an advertising-availability policy. Those protections are useful, but a buyer should still read the exact current terms before checkout.

10 · What the three sites mean for a small Solana memecoin

For ZombieCash and other small Solana projects, the best plan is not to marry one platform and send it the treasury. List for free where possible, build a repeatable community routine, then test paid placements with trackable links and a strict stop-loss for marketing spend.

  1. Establish the free footprint on all three. Use one consistent token name, ticker, contract address, logo, website and social links. Save each listing URL and check it on desktop and mobile.
  2. Make CoinVote the community engine. Publish the listing in Telegram and X, explain how daily votes work, use the vote badge on the site and encourage real members to participate. Avoid scripts, vote farms, duplicate accounts and artificial engagement. The point is to build a returning community, not to manufacture a screenshot.
  3. Use CoinSniper for discovery and optional credibility tools. Evaluate the listing, watchlist and owner dashboard first. Consider Boosts or KYC only after confirming the current cost, requirements and what the badge actually verifies.
  4. Use CoinMooner as a supplementary reach channel. Its directory breadth and ad formats may be useful, especially if the team wants banners, a promoted spot or an email campaign. Before paying, request current audience evidence, targeting breakdown, click reporting, contracting entity and invoice details.
  5. Track every campaign separately. Use unique UTM links for each platform and placement. Measure visits, engaged sessions, Telegram joins, returning visitors and meaningful actions. Do not count a vote as a buyer or a banner impression as a conversion.
  6. Run a small test before scaling. Start with the smallest available paid placement, compare the result against your own website analytics and community activity, and stop if the platform cannot provide the agreed reporting.

11 · Final verdict: three tools, three jobs

CoinSniper is a broad discovery platform with a substantial directory, ranking/Boost mechanics and optional KYC, audit and quest services. It is useful when the project wants additional visibility and a suite of owner-facing tools. The legal-operator details and current paid pricing deserve clarification before a purchase.

CoinMooner is a broad crypto portal with a deep menu of discovery categories and clearly published advertising formats. Its banner, pop-up, promoted spot, email and audit products are easy to understand commercially. But the platform's own audience/listing counters are inconsistent across public captures, and its published operating-entity documents are older than the reported liquidation status of one named company. That makes current contracting details a pre-payment requirement.

CoinVote stands out for actual community participation mechanics. Its free listing and voting model, published ranking formula, Nuggets, streaks, challenges, member profiles, Community Hub, mobile apps and public activity counters make it more than a billboard. The platform gives members recurring reasons to come back and participate. That is especially relevant to small Solana projects that need an active community rather than a one-day spike in page views.

THE ZOMBCASH CONCLUSION: Put CoinVote at the center of the community-engagement strategy. Use CoinSniper as an additional discovery and credibility channel. Use CoinMooner selectively for its directory reach and advertising inventory, after verifying current audience quality and the contracting entity. Keep all three free listings active if maintenance is worthwhile. Spend only when tracking proves that paid visibility creates real, returning people.

In the wasteland, the loudest billboard is not necessarily the safest shelter. The platform that helps people return, participate and recognize each other may be worth more than the one with the biggest number painted on its gate. And if someone promises “one million active buyers,” ask whether they mean buyers, visitors, votes, bots, or one very enthusiastic spreadsheet.
